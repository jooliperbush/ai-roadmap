# Miscited: market analysis and recommended position

Research date: 5 September 2026. This is desk research plus inspection of the working codebase and public GitHub metadata. No customers, revenue, conversion data, interview transcripts or launch budget were supplied. Recommendations, scores, prices and revenue scenarios below are hypotheses to test, not measured demand or forecasts.

## Recommendation

Position Miscited as **AI answer accuracy for B2B SaaS teams**. Sell a practical first outcome: identify an evidenced factual error, make a correction to a source the customer controls, then measure the follow-up. Recruit small specialist SEO/content agencies as a channel, starting with one client each.

The customer-facing promise is: **“Your product changed. The answer didn’t.”** The explanatory line is: “Find outdated prices, retired plans and unsupported claims. Trace the evidence, correct the source, and test whether the next answer gets it right.” This is a promise of a workflow; it is not a promise that a model will change or that revenue will increase.

Use familiar AEO/GEO language in discovery content, but make factual accuracy the product’s lead. Start alongside a customer’s visibility platform, spreadsheet or content workflow. Replacing an established SEO suite is a much harder initial sale.

## 1. What market this belongs to

| Layer | Buyer’s question | Miscited’s role |
|---|---|---|
| AI search visibility / AEO / GEO | Are assistants mentioning and recommending us? | Adjacent category and source of existing budgets |
| Answer accuracy and brand information quality | Are factual claims current, supported and attributable? | Primary product position |
| Content operations | What should we change, who approves it, and where? | Correction workflow and reviewable drafts |
| Experimentation and measurement | Did the result change beyond variation or model drift? | Follow-up evaluation with uncertainty |
| Enterprise governance | Can this meet procurement, compliance and organizational controls? | Possible later expansion; avoid leading here now |

“LLM observability” usually addresses an application builder’s own model calls. Miscited primarily samples external representations of a company. “Reputation management” includes subjective sentiment and crisis response; that is broader than the product’s evidence-based claims. “Fact checking” without qualification could imply independent truth verification, which the software does not provide merely by reading a customer’s own site.

## 2. Why the problem matters—and what the evidence does not prove

Pew’s analysis of 900 U.S. adults’ March 2025 browsing found traditional search-result clicks on 8% of visits with a Google AI summary versus 15% without; links inside the summary received clicks on 1% of visits with summaries. This makes the answer itself a relevant information surface. It is an observational Google study, not evidence that Miscited customers lose a particular percentage of deals, and not a 2026 B2B buyer survey. [Pew study and methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)

Supply-side evidence is stronger than buyer evidence: numerous vendors sell this category, and large suites now bundle or sell AI visibility. This validates competitive activity and budget categories, not willingness to pay specifically for accuracy. Peec’s own explanation of Actions describes users wanting concrete next steps after seeing dashboards. That is a vendor-reported customer signal, not independent interview research. [Peec’s introduction of Actions](https://peec.ai/blog/introducing-actions)

The key unknown is whether recurring factual errors create enough recurring work for a subscription. A correction sprint may be an easier purchase than indefinite monitoring. Validate both rather than assuming recurring software revenue.

The scores are judgment, 1–5; higher is more favorable. They are a prioritization aid rather than market statistics.

| Consumer e-commerce catalogs | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 16 |
| Very small/local businesses | 2 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 13 |

**Initial buyer:** head of product marketing, content or SEO at a SaaS company; founder at a smaller vendor. **Champion:** content/SEO operator or support lead who already sees misconceptions. **Approver:** marketing lead/founder. **Implementer:** content owner or engineer with access to the relevant page. Agencies can be champion, implementer and reseller, but require client permission and a clear division of review responsibility.

Qualify for a recent pricing/packaging/integration change, public documentation, observable relevant AI answers, an owner who can correct sources, and willingness to review evidence. Deprioritize prospects who want guaranteed mentions, cannot change any source, only want a one-off vanity score, or have no recurring information changes.

**First outreach list specification:** 30 SaaS companies with public recent product/pricing changes and 20 specialist agencies with relevant client case studies. This is a proposed list-building task; no prospect list or personal contact details were scraped in this work.

## 5. Differentiation grounded in the code

| Implemented evidence | Buyer benefit | Constraint to state |
|---|---|---|
| Dated canonical claims; approval/adjudication | Compare against an explicit record, including historical facts | Customer-provided facts are not independent verification |
| Run provenance and citation snapshots | Inspect what was asked and what was cited | Pages can be unavailable; extraction misses things |
| Separate intent families, sample floors and intervals | Avoid misleading aggregation and bare percentages | Five runs is a display floor, not adequate power for every inference |
| Actions, transitions, connector drafts and experiments | Keep correction and follow-up together | Draft creation is not publishing or guaranteed model influence |
| Repeated sampling, controls and experiment analysis | Distinguish evidence of change from a single answer | Causal claims depend on design, assumptions and enough data |
| Per-brand roles, portfolio, schedules, budgets | Fit an agency’s repeated workflow | Production integrations and deployment remain unverified |

The potential defensible asset is a **permissioned, longitudinal dataset of factual errors, interventions and outcomes**, combined with a usable workflow. It is not a moat yet. A larger vendor can copy a UI or statistical method. Repeatable outcome evidence, evaluation fixtures, user trust and distribution are harder to copy.

## 6. Packaging and willingness-to-pay experiments

The prior website’s $750/$2,000/$5,000 ladder was not supported by supplied customer evidence. It may work for service-heavy engagements, but it is a high entry hurdle against cheap monitoring. The new marketing page offers a free sample and individually scoped pilots; it does not implement billing. The internal methodology now labels the older plan prices as historical planning examples rather than current offers. Account creation still schedules monitoring, so settle usage budgets and commercial terms before a paid launch.

Test sequentially with comparable qualified prospects:

| Offer hypothesis | Test price, USD | Explicit deliverable and limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Free diagnostic | $0 | One domain, bounded sample, provisional findings and clear live/simulated label; qualify before spending substantial API budget |
| Correction sprint | $299 one time | One brand, up to 10 buyer-question clusters, one agreed market, up to 2 configured surfaces, a reviewed baseline, one correction plan and a follow-up readout; quote usage cap separately |
| Ongoing monitor | $149/month hypothesis | One brand, recurring bounded sampling and evidence review by the customer; no managed writing promise |
| Agency pilot | $499/month hypothesis | Up to 3 approved client brands, shared onboarding and a strict aggregate run cap; agency owns client fact review |

Do not publicly promise these until costs and delivery time have been observed. Test the sprint with 10 qualified prospects; record paid acceptance, objections, review time, actionable-finding rate and follow-up participation. A positive conversation is not willingness to pay. Avoid percentage-improvement guarantees or refund conditions tied to making a model change.

Unit economics example, **assumptions only**: 10 clusters × 2 surfaces × 5 repetitions × 4 rounds = 400 runs. At an assumed blended $0.02/run, API cost is $8; at $0.10 it is $40. Add fetch/storage, retries and staff time. Three hours of review at an internal $50/hour is $150, dominating model costs. A $299 sprint leaves $109–$141 before other costs in this illustration. Measure actual usage rather than treating $0.02 as a current provider quote. Do not promise unlimited scans.

## 7. Opportunity sizing without an invented TAM

No reliable count of companies actively buying answer-accuracy software was established. A headline global AEO market estimate would blend too many categories. Use a reachable-account model until discovery supplies a validated denominator.

An initial operating scenario: build a verified list of 200 qualified accounts; if 15% have a discovery conversation, 40% of those start a paid sprint and 50% continue monitoring, that produces 30 conversations, 12 sprints and 6 monitoring customers. At the test prices: $3,588 one-time sprint revenue and $894 MRR. These conversion assumptions have no observed baseline yet.

| Steady-state scenario, not forecast | SaaS customers × $149/month | Agencies × $499/month | MRR | Annualized run-rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small founder business | 20 | 5 | $5,475 | $65,700 |
| Established niche | 75 | 15 | $18,660 | $223,920 |
| Expanded distribution | 200 | 40 | $49,760 | $597,120 |

Annualized run-rate is MRR × 12, not recognized revenue; churn, onboarding capacity, sales cycle, discounts, tax and costs are excluded. Avoid double-counting agency client brands as separate direct customers. These scenarios show what must be sold, not how much market exists.

## 8. Principal risks and experiments

| Risk | Consequence | Test or response |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy is a low-frequency problem | Subscription churn | Compare repeat purchase/monitoring interest after a correction sprint |
| Customer facts are disputed or stale | False confidence | Require named fact owner and date; show provisional status |
| Models do not change after corrections | Weak perceived value | Include unresolved outcomes; measure time-to-diagnosis and correction work, not only model movement |
| API answers differ from consumer apps | Buyer challenges validity | Label surface; show setup; test the buyer’s required surface before promising coverage |
| Suite vendors absorb the workflow | Distribution/cost disadvantage | Specialize in a documented vertical, partner with operators and export evidence |
| Free audit abuse or high cost | Cash burn | Enforce deployed budget/rate limits; observe real run cost and failures |
| Unproven production posture | Launch disappointment | Verify provider keys, data handling, background jobs and payment/offer consistency before promotion |
| Stars mistaken for demand | Wrong priorities | Track activated teams, reviewed findings and paid continuation alongside stars |

## 9. Discovery and decision gates

Interview 10 SaaS operators and 5 agency leads. Ask for the last wrong AI answer they actually saw; what fact was wrong; what they did; who could fix the source; what they spent; how they checked the result; and what would make this worth buying again. Ask to inspect a concrete example with permission. Avoid “Would you use this?” hypotheticals.

Suggested internal gates, not benchmarks: at least 5 of 15 interviewees show a material recurring problem; at least 3 of 10 qualified sprint offers are paid; at least 2 pilots complete a reviewed correction and return for follow-up. If failures are mostly lack of demand, change segment/offer before building more. If buyers pay but reviews take too long, improve onboarding and fact import. If they use the tool once and leave satisfied, sell periodic sprints rather than forcing a subscription.
